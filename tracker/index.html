      How the dates and statuses work

      This tracker is a reminder aid. It does not check that your certificates are valid and it is not legal advice. Check your certificate for the exact date. You can always type your own due date instead of a suggested one.

      Statuses

      • Overdue the date has passed.
      • Due within 60 days the date is today or in the next 60 days.
      • OK the date is more than 60 days away (or the deposit is marked as protected).
      • Missing no date has been entered yet.

      Gas safety check

      The law says landlords must make sure each gas appliance and flue is checked for safety at intervals of not more than 12 months (Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998, regulation 36). If you only enter the date of your last check, the tracker suggests the day before its 1-year anniversary, marked “suggested”.

      There is also a rule that can let a check carried out up to 2 months early keep its original due date (regulation 36A), and different appliances can have different dates. The tracker does not apply that rule, so if your record shows a later due date, enter it yourself.

      Electrical inspection (EICR)

      Electrical installations must be inspected and tested at intervals of no more than 5 years, or sooner if the most recent report says so (Electrical Safety Standards Regulations 2020, regulation 3; gov.uk guidance). Each report gives the date by which the next inspection is required, so enter that date. If you leave it empty, the tracker suggests the day before the 5-year anniversary of the inspection, marked “suggested”. An earlier date on your report always applies.

      Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)

      gov.uk says an EPC is valid for 10 years (gov.uk: Energy Performance Certificates). The tracker doesn’t work this out: enter the expiry date shown on your certificate.

      Deposit protection

      gov.uk says you (or your letting agent) must put your tenants’ deposit in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of getting it (gov.uk: deposit protection schemes; Housing Act 2004, section 213). If you mark a deposit as not yet protected and enter the date you received it, the tracker shows the last day of that 30-day period, counting the day you received it. Check gov.uk for which tenancies this applies to.
